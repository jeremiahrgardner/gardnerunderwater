**San Diego is one of the best places in the country to learn DIR and technical diving skills.** Cold water, changing visibility and real conditions build divers who can hold position, manage a drysuit and dive as a team. Training sites range from easy beach entries at La Jolla Shores to the La Jolla canyon, the Point Loma kelp forest and Wreck Alley off Mission Beach.

Plenty of divers learn in warm, clear, calm water and struggle the first time conditions change. Divers who learn here don't have that problem. If you can hold trim and buoyancy in a 7 mm suit or a drysuit, in 15 feet of visibility, you can do it anywhere.

## What are diving conditions like in San Diego?

San Diego water is cool year-round and visibility varies with the season, swell and plankton. Typical ranges, according to the local resource [Divebums](https://divebums.com/local-info/visitor-info-general-conditions.html):

| Season | Surface temperature | Temperature at depth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Summer | High 60s to low 70s °F | Low 60s to high 60s °F, sometimes low 50s |
| Winter | High 50s to low 60s °F | Very low 50s to high 50s °F, occasionally 49 °F |

- **Visibility:** shore dives around La Jolla average roughly 10 to 20 feet. It can drop to a couple of feet or open up to 30 or 40. Offshore boat dives are usually clearer.
- **Plankton blooms:** in late spring and early summer, blooms can make the top layer murky while the water below stays clearer.
- **Exposure protection:** many local divers use drysuits. Wetsuit divers typically wear 6 or 7 mm, with a hood and thick gloves.

These conditions are exactly why San Diego is good for training. You learn buoyancy with real exposure suits, navigation with real visibility and team awareness when your teammate isn't always in plain view.

## Where do DIR and technical divers train in San Diego?

### La Jolla Shores

A wide sandy beach entry with a gradual slope, making it the most common training site in the county. The sand flats are ideal for buoyancy, trim and propulsion drills. A short swim takes you to the edge of the La Jolla canyon, where the bottom drops away and depth comes quickly. The fine bottom also tells you instantly whether your kicks are silting.

### La Jolla Cove

Part of the protected underwater park, with rocky reef, kelp and abundant marine life. Conditions depend heavily on swell and the entry can be tricky, so it suits divers who are already comfortable.

### Point Loma kelp forest

A boat dive through one of the region's largest kelp forests. It's a good place to practice awareness and positioning: staying close to your team while moving through kelp without getting tangled.

### Wreck Alley

A collection of artificial reefs and wrecks off Mission Beach, including the Yukon, a former Canadian navy destroyer escort. Wrecks bring depth, current and structure: good ground for gas planning, ascent discipline and team position, and a natural next step toward wreck training.

## What should you know before diving here?

- **Swell and surf matter.** Check the surf forecast before shore dives. A beautiful day can still have a rough entry.
- **Plan for cold.** Your weighting, your gas consumption and your dexterity all change in cold water. Do a proper weight check with your actual suit; see [How to Master Scuba Buoyancy](/articles/scuba-buoyancy-control).
- **Plan your gas for the swim back.** Shore dives that start with a surface swim often call for halves or thirds. See [Gas Management for Recreational Divers](/articles/scuba-gas-management).
- **Dive with a team that's trained the same way.** It matters more in low visibility.

## Where do technical divers get gas in San Diego?

Technical diving needs reliable nitrox, oxygen and helium fills and a shop that understands doubles and stage bottles.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a drysuit to dive in San Diego?

No, but many local divers use one, especially in winter or for longer and deeper dives. A 7 mm wetsuit with a hood and gloves works for many divers in summer.

### What is the best time of year to dive San Diego?

Summer and early fall usually bring the warmest water. Visibility depends on swell and plankton and can be good in any season.

### Is San Diego a good place to learn technical diving?

Yes. Accessible depth, cold water and variable conditions build strong fundamentals, and the training transfers to warmer, clearer destinations.
