**A DIR gear configuration is a standard way of setting up scuba equipment so that every item sits in the same place, on every diver, on every dive.** It uses a backplate and wing, a one-piece harness, a long-hose primary regulator, a bungeed backup and as little else as possible. The goal isn't looks. It's that you, or a teammate, can find and use anything without thinking.

Most gear problems underwater aren't failures. They're fumbles: a reg you can't find, a hose you snag, a clip you can't open in gloves. A standard configuration removes the fumbles.

## Why standardize your gear at all?

Standardization turns emergencies into routines. If your teammate's backup regulator is always under their chin, you never search for it. If everyone's gas is set up the same way, anyone can help with anyone's problem.

It also scales. The same layout works from a single aluminum tank in warm water to doubles with stage bottles in a cave. Divers learn it once and build on it, rather than relearning a new setup at each step.

## The configuration at a glance

| Item | Standard placement | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Backplate | Rigid plate on your back, steel or aluminum | Stable platform; can replace belt weight |
| Harness | One continuous piece of 2-inch webbing, one buckle | Nothing to break, nothing to adjust mid-dive |
| Wing | Behind you, sized to the rig | Lift where it supports horizontal trim |
| Primary regulator | Long hose, 5 to 7 ft, routed around the neck | The reg you breathe is the one you donate |
| Backup regulator | Short hose, bungee necklace under the chin | Always within reach, hands-free |
| Pressure gauge | Short hose, clipped at the left hip | Easy to read, never dangling |
| Inflator hose | Left side | Matches the wing's inflator |
| Primary light | Canister on the right hip (when used) | Also holds the long hose in place |
| Backup light | Clipped to a chest D-ring | One for open water, two for overhead dives |
| Cutting tool | On the waist belt | Reachable with either hand |
| Stage and deco bottles | Clipped on the left side | Keeps the long hose side clear |

## The backplate

The backplate is the foundation. It holds the cylinder or doubles tight against your back so the tank can't roll, and it puts some of your ballast high and centered.

- **Stainless steel** plates are heavier. In cold water or a drysuit, a steel plate can replace much of the lead you'd otherwise wear on a belt.
- **Aluminum** plates are lighter. They suit warm water, thin suits and travel.

The plate should sit high enough that you can reach your valves, without the top of the tank hitting the back of your head.

## The harness

A DIR harness is a single continuous length of 2-inch webbing threaded through the plate, with one stainless steel buckle at the right waist and a crotch strap. D-rings sit at the left and right shoulders, the left hip, and on the crotch strap.

There are no plastic buckles, no quick releases and no padding to compress. Once it's adjusted to your body, you never touch it again. It should be snug enough to hold the plate in place but loose enough to reach your valves and breathe deeply.

## The wing

The wing is the buoyancy cell behind you. Size it to the rig, not to the biggest dive you might ever do. A wing that's too large traps air in corners, is harder to vent and adds drag.

Single-tank wings and doubles wings are different products. One wing rarely does both jobs well.

## The regulators

This is the heart of the configuration.

- **Primary on a long hose.** The second stage you breathe is on a 5- to 7-foot hose. It runs down your right side, under your light canister or a hip pocket, across your chest and around the back of your neck to your mouth. In an out-of-air emergency, you donate the regulator you're breathing. The full routing is in [Long Hose Configuration](/articles/long-hose-configuration).
- **Backup on a necklace.** Your backup second stage is on a short hose, held just under your chin by a loop of surgical tubing or bungee. When you donate the long hose, you drop your chin and the backup is right there.
- **Gauge on the left.** Your pressure gauge sits on a short hose, clipped to the left hip D-ring.

In backmounted doubles, the long hose comes off the right cylinder valve. The backup regulator, pressure gauge and wing inflator come off the left. That split lets you identify and isolate a failing regulator by feel.

## Lights, tools and storage

- **Primary light:** a canister light on the right hip, with the head on a Goodman handle. On open-water dives without a canister, a pocket or knife on the right hip does the job of holding the long hose.
- **Backup lights:** one small backup light clipped to a chest D-ring in open water, two for overhead diving.
- **Cutting tool:** on the waist belt, reachable with either hand.
- **Storage:** a thigh pocket on a drysuit or a small pocket on the harness for an SMB, spool and wet notes. Nothing clipped where it dangles.
- **Stage and deco bottles:** always on the left side. The right side stays clear so the long hose can deploy cleanly, and there's no right hip D-ring to clip one to anyway.

## Fins, mask and computer

- **Fins:** stiff blade fins with spring straps suit the frog kick and back kick. Split fins struggle with both, and UTD's propulsion training requires non-split fins. See [Scuba Finning Techniques](/articles/scuba-finning-techniques).
- **Mask:** low-volume, black skirt optional, with a spare on longer dives.
- **Computer:** wrist-mounted, where you can read it while holding position.

## What does every UTD course require?

UTD's standards set the same minimum kit for every open-circuit course, whether you dive backmount or sidemount, single tank or doubles:

- A primary regulator on a 5- or 7-foot hose wrapped around your neck, and a backup regulator on a bungee necklace.
- A rear-inflation buoyancy compensator: a wing on a backplate and harness, a back-inflate BCD, or a sidemount trim device. A traditional jacket BCD doesn't qualify.
- A depth gauge, a timer and a compass.
- A mask, and fins suited to UTD propulsion techniques.
- At least one cutting tool.
- A spool with at least 70 feet of line and a surface marker buoy.
- A primary light and a backup light, with two backups for overhead diving.
- Wet notes or a slate.
- An exposure suit suited to the dive.

On an entry-level open water course, the spool, SMB, lights and wet notes are optional.

## Common configuration mistakes

- **Clipping things "just in case."** Every extra item is drag and something to snag. Take it off or put it in a pocket.
- **Long hose with nothing to hold it at the hip.** It loops away from your body. Use a canister, a pocket or a knife sheath to retain it.
- **A loose backup necklace.** The backup should sit right under your chin, not hang at your chest.
- **Overweighting to "be safe."** A steel plate plus a full belt plus a steel tank is a lot of lead. Do a proper end-of-dive weight check; see [How to Master Scuba Buoyancy](/articles/scuba-buoyancy-control).
- **Buying first, training second.** Dive a configured rig with an instructor before you buy one.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use a DIR configuration with a single tank?

Yes. A single tank uses the same layout: long hose primary, bungeed backup, gauge on the left and a wing sized for one cylinder.

### Is a backplate and wing better than a jacket BCD?

For trim, stability and standardization, most divers find it is. A jacket has advantages at the surface. We compare them in [Backplate and Wing vs Jacket BCD](/articles/backplate-and-wing-vs-jacket-bcd).

### Do I need a canister light for recreational diving?

No. On daytime open-water dives, a hip pocket or knife sheath can hold the long hose. A canister light becomes standard for overhead and night diving.

### Can I dive steel doubles in a wetsuit?

Not in UTD training. UTD's standards only allow steel doubles when you're also wearing a drysuit, which gives you a second source of buoyancy if your wing fails. Steel cylinders that are neutral or positive at 500 psi are the exception.

### Is sidemount compatible with DIR?

UTD's system brings sidemount into the same logic, with the same long hose and backup layout. That's covered in our [Doubles (Backmount or Sidemount)](/courses/doubles-backmount-or-sidemount) course.
