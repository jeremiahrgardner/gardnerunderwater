**Five kicks give you full control underwater: the frog kick, the modified frog, the modified flutter, the helicopter turn and the back kick.** Together they let you move forward, creep through tight spaces, turn in place and back up, all without changing depth or stirring up the bottom. The standard flutter kick most divers learn first does only one of those jobs well.

If you only know the flutter kick, you can swim. These five let you control exactly where you are.

## Why does kicking technique matter?

Every kick pushes water somewhere. The flutter kick pushes it down toward the bottom and up toward the surface. That stirs up silt, damages reefs and nudges you up and down with every stroke.

The kicks below push water behind you, or barely move water at all. That gives you three things:

- **Precision:** you stop where you mean to stop.
- **Efficiency:** a glide after each kick means fewer strokes and less gas.
- **Respect for the site:** sand stays on the bottom and the reef stays intact.

They all depend on horizontal trim. If your feet hang below you, these kicks push you up instead of forward. See [Horizontal Trim](/articles/horizontal-trim-feet-sinking).

## The five kicks at a glance

| Kick | What it does | Best for | Silt risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Frog kick | Efficient forward propulsion with a glide | Cruising, most of the dive | Low |
| Modified frog | Small, gentle forward movement | Near the bottom, close to other divers | Very low |
| Modified flutter | Tiny flutter from the knees and ankles | Tight spaces, very silty bottoms | Very low |
| Helicopter turn | Rotates you in place | Turning to face your team or exit | Very low |
| Back kick | Moves you backward | Holding position, backing away, ascents | Very low |

## The frog kick

The frog kick is the workhorse. Your knees stay bent and your fins stay high, roughly level with your hips.

1. Start with knees bent about 90 degrees and fins together, soles facing up.
2. Rotate your ankles so the fins point outward, then sweep them out and back in a wide arc.
3. Snap the fins together behind you to finish the stroke.
4. Glide. Let the kick carry you before starting the next.

The power comes from the sweep, not from straightening your legs. The glide is where you save gas.

## The modified frog

The modified frog is a smaller version of the same motion, driven mostly from the knees and ankles. Use it close to the bottom, in a group, or anywhere a full kick would be too much.

## The modified flutter

The modified flutter keeps your knees bent and moves only your lower legs and ankles in small strokes. Your fins stay high and your thighs barely move. It's the kick for very tight places and very fine silt, where even a small frog kick would cloud the water.

## The helicopter turn

The helicopter turn rotates you on the spot without moving forward or back. One fin does a small forward frog motion while the other does a small reverse motion, turning you around your own center. Use it to face your team, check behind you or turn toward the exit.

## The back kick

The back kick moves you backward, which is how you hold position in current, back away from a reef or a photo subject, and stay in place on ascents. It's the hardest of the five to learn and the most useful once you have it.

It gets its own step-by-step guide: [How to Back Kick](/articles/how-to-back-kick).

## Which fins work best?

Stiff blade fins with spring straps work best for these kicks. They hold their shape on the sweep of the frog kick and give you something to push against on the back kick. Split fins and very soft fins are designed for flutter kicking and struggle with all five.

Fin weight also affects trim. Heavy fins pull your feet down in a wetsuit; in a drysuit they can help offset air that collects in your legs.

## How to learn these kicks

- **Start with the frog kick.** Make it smooth before you try the others.
- **Practice without fins first.** In a pool, the motions are easier to feel without fin resistance.
- **Go slow.** These are technique kicks, not power kicks. Rushing defeats them.
- **Get filmed.** You can't see your own fins. A few seconds of video shows what a dozen dives of guessing won't.

## Common mistakes

- **Dropping the knees.** Fins drift below your body and every kick pushes you up.
- **Skipping the glide.** You work twice as hard for the same distance.
- **Kicking from the hips.** These kicks come from the knees and ankles.
- **Using your hands to steer.** Turns should come from your fins, with a helicopter turn.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the frog kick better than the flutter kick?

For most diving, yes. It's more efficient, quieter on the bottom and easier to control. The flutter kick still has a place for short bursts against current in open water.

### Can I frog kick in split fins?

You can try, but split fins aren't built for it. Most divers find the frog kick and back kick far easier in stiff blade fins.

### How long does it take to learn the back kick?

It varies a lot. Some divers get a working back kick in a single session with coaching; others take many dives. Practicing without fins and getting video feedback shortens the process.
