**If your feet sink when you stop kicking, your center of weight is below your center of lift.** The fix is usually to move weight higher, toward your chest, and to change your posture: knees bent, fins up, back gently arched and head looking forward. Adding air or kicking harder only hides the problem.

Feet-down trim is the most common position problem in recreational diving. It costs you gas, pushes you up with every kick and stirs up the bottom. The good news is that it's mechanical. Fix the mechanics and the trim follows.

## What is horizontal trim?

Trim is your body angle in the water. Horizontal trim means lying roughly flat, parallel to the bottom, without effort. You can hold it while hovering still, not just while swimming.

Why it matters:

- **Your kicks push you forward,** not up toward the surface.
- **You present less drag,** so you work less and breathe less.
- **You keep the bottom clean,** because your fins are above you, not below.
- **You can see your team and where you're going.**

## Why do my feet sink?

Your body rotates around the point where weight and lift balance. If weight sits low, your feet drop. Common causes, roughly in order of how often we see them:

1. **Lead on your hips.** A weight belt or integrated pockets put most of your ballast at your waist.
2. **The tank sits too low.** The cylinder's weight pulls your lower body down.
3. **Straight legs.** Extended legs put your heavy fins far from your center and act like a lever.
4. **Looking down.** Your body follows your head.
5. **Heavy fins.** Some fins are very negative, which matters most in a thin wetsuit.
6. **Air in your drysuit legs.** This usually makes feet float, but shifting air causes trim to change through the dive.
7. **Overweighting.** More lead means more air in the wing, which makes everything less stable.

## How do I diagnose my own trim?

Do a static test. At a comfortable depth over sand:

1. Get neutral and horizontal.
2. Stop all movement: hands still, fins still.
3. Count to 30.
4. Notice what moves first. Do your feet drop? Does your head dip? Do you roll?

Have your buddy film it. Five seconds of video tells you more than a dozen dives of guessing.

## How do I fix feet-down trim?

Work through these in order. Each change is small; make one at a time.

### 1. Fix your posture

- Bend your knees to about 90 degrees, so your fins sit up behind you.
- Arch your back gently and push your chest down slightly.
- Look forward, not down.
- Keep your arms in front of you, hands clasped or holding a light.

Posture alone fixes trim for many divers.

### 2. Move weight up

- **Raise the tank** in the cam bands, until the valve almost touches the back of your head.
- **Use a steel backplate** or a weight on the plate instead of a belt.
- **Add small trim weights** high on the cylinder, on the cam band, instead of at your waist.

Move weight, don't add it.

### 3. Reduce total weight

Do an end-of-dive weight check. Less lead means less air in your wing and less to balance. See [How to Master Scuba Buoyancy](/articles/scuba-buoyancy-control).

### 4. Check your fins

If your fins are very negative and you dive a thin wetsuit, a lighter fin can help. In a drysuit, heavier fins often help keep your feet from floating.

### 5. Manage drysuit air

Keep only enough air in your suit to avoid squeeze, and use your wing for buoyancy. Too much suit air migrates to your feet and shoulders as you change position.

## What about head-down trim?

Less common, but it happens, usually from weight placed too high or too much weight on the plate. The fixes run in reverse: move weight lower, toward your hips, and check that you aren't looking straight down.

## Trim and the rest of your diving

Horizontal trim is what makes good propulsion possible. The frog kick and back kick only work properly when you're flat. See [Scuba Finning Techniques](/articles/scuba-finning-techniques) and [How to Back Kick](/articles/how-to-back-kick).

It also makes a backplate and wing worth having. A wing puts lift along your back, where it supports flat trim. See [Backplate and Wing vs Jacket BCD](/articles/backplate-and-wing-vs-jacket-bcd).

## Common trim mistakes

- **Kicking to stay horizontal.** If you have to kick to stay flat, your trim is off.
- **Adding air to lift your feet.** Air rises to the highest point of your wing, not your feet.
- **Fixing everything at once.** Change one thing, test it, then change the next.
- **Ignoring the video.** What feels flat often isn't.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need ankle weights to fix trim?

Usually not, and they're a fix for floating feet, not sinking ones. Moving weight and fixing posture solves most trim problems.

### Why is my trim fine while swimming but not when I stop?

Kicking hides poor trim. The test is whether you can hold position while completely still.

### How flat should I be?

Roughly parallel to the bottom, with your knees bent and fins up. Small variations are normal; the goal is a position you can hold without effort.

### Does a drysuit make trim harder?

At first, because air moves inside the suit. With correct weighting and minimal suit air, many divers find trim easier in a drysuit.
