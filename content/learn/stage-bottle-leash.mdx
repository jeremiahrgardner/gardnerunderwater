One bottle is easy. Two takes some thought.

Three or more, and the question isn't whether you can carry them. It's whether you can find the right one, switch to it, pass it to a teammate and clip it back, without turning your left side into a junk drawer.

Most of that is decided before you get in the water. By where everything goes.

## Why does everything go on the left?

Because your right side is spoken for.

Your long hose runs down your right side. When a teammate is out of gas, it has to come free in one movement. Clip a bottle on the right and you've put something in the way of the most important hose on your rig.

So bottles go left. Every bottle. Every diver. See [DIR Gear Configuration](/learn/dir-gear-configuration) for how the rest of the rig fits around them.

## Which bottle goes inside?

UTD's convention is simple: **stage inside, deco outside.**

Here's the thing. The order matters less than the fact that everyone uses the same one. When your teammate glances at your left side, they already know which bottle is which. When they hand one back, they know where it goes.

Standardization isn't a style choice. It's how a team reads each other at depth.

## When do you need a leash?

When you're carrying more bottles than you can sling cleanly. Extra bottles ride on a short leash on your left side, close to your body, so they don't swing or drag.

How the leash is set up, what clips where and how bottles move on and off it during the dive is what we train with you in the water. It's simple. It's also exactly the kind of detail that goes wrong when it's learned from a diagram.

## Doesn't more gas mean more margin?

Let's be honest. It's tempting to think so.

**The comfortable belief:** every bottle I add is more margin.

**The truth:** every bottle you add is another gas you could breathe at the wrong depth. Another valve that can fail. Another regulator to stow, another clip to snag, another thing your team has to track.

More bottles only add margin if you can manage them. Otherwise they add risk.

That's why gas planning comes first. [Scuba Gas Management](/learn/scuba-gas-management) and the [Rule of Thirds](/learn/rule-of-thirds-diving) decide what you carry. The bottles just carry it.

## Can your teammate read your left side?

Picture your team at the first gas switch. Could your teammate point to each of your bottles and name the gas? Could they hand one back and know exactly where it clips?

If not, it's not their problem. It's a setup problem.

Bottle handling, gas switching and passing bottles between divers are the heart of the [Stage and/or Deco Bottle Extension](/courses/stage-and-or-deco-bottle-extension). Start with one bottle. Get it right. Then add the next.
