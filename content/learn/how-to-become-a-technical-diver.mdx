The most common mistake we see is the reverse: a diver buys doubles, signs up for a deco course and discovers in the water that their foundations can't carry the load. Technical diving doesn't forgive weak basics. It magnifies them.

## What is technical diving?

UTD defines its technical training category as dives deeper than 100 feet/30 meters with planned decompression and no overhead environment. Its overhead training is a separate category. Recreational training can extend to 130 feet/39 meters within Min Deco limits and the diver's qualification, using 25/25 beyond 100 feet. Helium use alone does not make a dive technical.

In wider diving usage, technical diving can involve one or more of these:

- **Decompression:** dives that require mandatory stops on the way up, so you can't ascend directly to the surface.
- **Overhead environments:** caves and wreck interiors, where you can't ascend at all until you've swum out.
- **Mixed gases:** helium mixes (trimix) to limit narcosis at depth, and nitrox 50 or oxygen for decompression.
- **More equipment:** doubles or sidemount, stage and decompression bottles, and redundant systems.

Planned decompression and overhead environments remove a direct ascent as the normal exit strategy. Gas mixtures and extra equipment support those plans; they do not by themselves define the category. You need the training, gas and team skills for the actual dive.

## Are you ready to start?

Before any technical course, you should be able to do these reliably, not just occasionally:

- Maintain depth and horizontal trim while communicating, checking gas or deploying an SMB. Readiness includes awareness and team performance, not just a single numerical depth tolerance.
- Stay horizontal and still, without using your hands.
- Frog kick, back kick and helicopter turn without stirring up the bottom.
- Donate your long hose and share gas calmly while holding position.
- Plan your gas, including rock bottom and turn pressure, before the dive.

If any of those feels shaky, that's not a reason to stop. It's a clear list of what to work on first. See [How to Master Scuba Buoyancy](/learn/scuba-buoyancy-control), [Scuba Finning Techniques](/learn/scuba-finning-techniques) and [Gas Management for Recreational Divers](/learn/scuba-gas-management).

## The training path, step by step

| Step | Course | What it builds | Depth and gas limits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Essentials of Recreational Diving | Buoyancy through breathing, horizontal trim, five kicks, gas sharing, valve drills, SMB, rock bottom planning | 60 ft (100 ft if nitrox certified); no decompression |
| 2 | Essentials of Tech | Advanced buoyancy, trim and propulsion; valve shutdown; handling one deco bottle; advanced gas planning and ascents | 60 ft; air or nitrox 32; no decompression |
| As needed | Doubles (Backmount or Sidemount) | Normal and emergency skills for double-tank diving, including valve drills | Course-specific |
| 3 | Techreational | Entry to decompression diving with trimix 25/25 and one oxygen bottle | 130 ft / 39 m; one oxygen cycle |
| 4 | Tech 1 | Trimix 25/25, 21/35 or 18/45; one oxygen or nitrox 50 deco bottle; stage bottle only with training | 160 ft / 48 m; O2: one 15-minute cycle; nitrox 50: 30 min deco |

### 1. Essentials of Recreational Diving

This is where the foundation gets built, and it's UTD's entry point for divers already certified by another agency. You learn buoyancy control through breathing, horizontal trim, five non-silting kicks, gas sharing with a long hose, valve drills, SMB deployment and rock bottom gas planning, normally over three days with video debriefs. Every later course assumes these skills. [Course details](/courses/essentials-of-recreational-diving).

### 2. Essentials of Tech

A personal skills class that prepares you for technical depths. It takes place in shallow open water, stays within no-decompression limits, and advances your buoyancy, trim and propulsion, including the back kick and helicopter turn. It introduces valve shutdowns, more advanced air sharing, handling one decompression bottle, and more advanced gas planning and ascent strategies. It's also useful for recreational divers who simply want to be more skilled. More in [UTD Essentials of Tech](/learn/utd-essentials-of-tech), and how the training dives work in [Personal Skills, Critical Skills and Experience Dives](/learn/utd-critical-skills-dives). [Course details](/courses/essentials-of-tech).

### Doubles, when you need it

Doubles change your buoyancy, your trim and your emergency procedures. This course trains the normal and emergency skills every double-tank diver needs, in either backmount or sidemount, including shutting down and reopening a valve quickly. Take it when you're ready to move into doubles, before you need them for a technical course. [Course details](/courses/doubles).

### 3. Techreational

UTD's first step into technical diving. You learn to dive normoxic trimix 25/25 to 130 feet with one decompression bottle of oxygen, in doubles. Despite its conservative limits, it's demanding: the focus is precision, awareness and team performance under pressure, through dives with real objectives and unexpected failures, on the bottom and in mid-water. Come in already comfortable with the frog kick, back kick, valve drills and gas switching. [Course details](/courses/techreational).

### 4. Tech 1

A further step in technical training. It extends your range to 160 feet/48 meters with trimix 25/25, 21/35 or 18/45 and one decompression bottle of oxygen or nitrox 50. Oxygen decompression is limited to one 15-minute oxygen cycle; nitrox 50 decompression is limited to 30 minutes. A stage bottle requires the stage MINI or equivalent stage training. Training focuses heavily on mid-water control, ascent discipline, gas switches, valve drills and handling failures away from the bottom. [Course details](/courses/tech-1).

## What are the prerequisites?

Under UTD's standards, each step has an age minimum, a training prerequisite and a minimum number of logged dives beyond open water certification:

| Course | Minimum age | Prior training | Dives beyond open water |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Essentials of Recreational Diving | 15; doubles and nitrox from 16 | Open water certification from a recognized agency | None set |
| Essentials of Tech | 18 | Advanced (UTD Rec 2) or equivalent | 50, including 25 non-training dives |
| Doubles | 16 | Advanced (UTD Rec 2) or equivalent | None set |
| Techreational | 21 | Essentials of Tech and nitrox, or equivalent | 75 (25 after Essentials of Tech recommended) |
| Tech 1 | 21 | Essentials of Tech, Rec 2 with Doubles MINI, Techreational, or equivalent with assessment | 75 |

Essentials of Tech, Techreational and Tech 1 also require swim tests, a 10-minute tow of a diver in full gear, and a rescue of a diver simulating oxygen toxicity. These are selected prerequisites, not the complete enrollment checklist. Your instructor verifies the general prerequisites, fitness requirements and any equivalency assessment. Certification is earned through demonstrated performance; paying for training does not guarantee a card.

## Do you need to buy doubles first?

No. Don't buy a technical rig before you've trained in one. Your instructor can help you find out which configuration, plate, wing and cylinders actually suit your body and your diving. Buying first is the most expensive way to learn what you needed.

See [DIR Gear Configuration, Piece by Piece](/learn/dir-gear-configuration) for how the system fits together.

## How long does it take?

It depends far more on practice than on calendar time. The dive minimums above give a rough idea, but each course gives you skills; the dives between courses make them automatic. Expect to dive regularly between steps, and expect an honest instructor to tell you when you need more time.

## Common mistakes on the path to tech

- **Chasing depth before control.** The certification card says what you're allowed to do, not what you can do well.
- **Skipping the fundamentals course.** Almost every technical skill is a fundamental skill under more load.
- **Training with a different system at each level.** Changing configuration and procedures between courses means relearning instead of building.
- **Treating failures as something that happens to other people.** Technical training is mostly practice for things going wrong.

The best first step depends on where your skills are today. [Essentials of Tech](/courses/essentials-of-tech) is the bridge from recreational to technical diving. If you're newer to the UTD system, start with [Essentials of Recreational Diving](/courses/essentials-of-recreational-diving).
