Twenty-five minutes into the dive, the plan changes.

The wreck is better than anyone expected. The team stays five minutes longer. The average depth comes out shallower than you planned. So what's your decompression now?

If your answer is "I'll check my computer," here's the follow-up. Which computer? Yours, or your teammate's? The one set to 30/70, or the one set to 50/80?

Ratio deco is the answer to that problem. Not a better computer. A better plan, carried in every diver's head.

## What is ratio deco?

Ratio deco is a decompression strategy. UTD describes it as a methodology that lets a diver apply existing decompression models as one cohesive strategy the whole team can run during the dive. Decompression on the fly.

The core idea is simple enough to say in one sentence. **At a given depth, your decompression time is a ratio of your bottom time.** The deeper you go, the more decompression each minute of bottom time costs you.

Around that idea sit a few simple rules about the shape of the ascent and where the gas switches happen. Simple enough to run in your head at depth. Simple enough that every diver on the team gets the same answer.

That's the point.

## What is it based on?

Not on a hunch. On a century of decompression research.

UTD's Ratio Deco course starts with the history: Haldane, the US Navy tables, Bühlmann's dissolved-gas model, and the bubble models such as VPM and RGBM. Ratio deco takes the profiles those models produce and simplifies them into rules a person can apply without a laptop.

So it isn't a new theory competing with Bühlmann. It sits on top of the models, the same way a pilot's rules of thumb sit on top of aerodynamics.

## Strategy vs. algorithm: what's the difference?

Here's the thing most people miss.

**An algorithm** is math a computer runs continuously. It tracks one diver's depth, second by second, through a model of how tissues load and unload gas. It's precise. It's also private. Your computer knows your profile and your settings. It knows nothing about your team.

**A strategy** is a set of rules people apply together. It's built from what the algorithms produce, but it lives in the team's heads, not in one diver's wrist. Everyone knows the plan before the dive. Everyone can recalculate it when the dive changes. Nobody's waiting for a screen to tell them what happens next.

UTD puts the goal plainly: plan decompression without primary reliance on tables or computers, and build a thinking decompression diver who can adjust when the profile changes. That's also why I teach with computers in [gauge mode](/learn/dive-computer-gauge-mode).

## What does ratio deco need to work?

It isn't a shortcut, and it isn't for self-study. UTD is explicit that it depends on:

- **Standard gases.** Bottom gas and deco gas are always the same mixes, so the rules always apply. See [Standard Gases](/learn/standard-gases).
- **A real team.** Everyone runs the same plan and checks each other.
- **Situational awareness.** You track time, depth, average depth and gas the whole dive.
- **Discipline.** You stay inside its limits, every time.

Take any of those away and the strategy stops working.

## Is ratio deco proven?

Ratio Deco has a record of use in DIR technical diving, but accumulated experience is not comparative clinical validation. A [2017 study of one trimix profile](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28357819/) compared bubble and inflammatory markers across schedules; it does not establish the safety of every Ratio Deco variant. The method has also changed over time. UTD Standards v7.0 lists the Ratio Deco 3.0 academic program, but the detailed stop-distribution examples available here are from historical UTD decks. A new methodology version should not be described as proven safer without supporting comparative evidence.

That's not a reason to dismiss it. It's a reason to learn it properly, from the people who maintain it, and to respect its limits.

## How do you learn it?

Not from me in a blog post. And not from a forum thread.

[UTD v7.0 §3-200](https://utdscubadiving.com/courses/utd-standards-and-procedures/lessons/standards-and-procedures-section-3-2-extensions-and-essentials/topic/3-200-ratio-deco-3-0/) describes **Ratio Deco 3.0 as academic-only**. The academic course does not itself include in-water evaluation or confer operational staged-decompression privileges. Current technical-course training, applicable procedures and supervised practical work remain distinct.

Then come put it in the water. The theory is the easy part. Running it at the end of a real dive, with your team, while the plan changes, is the skill.
