Watch most divers stop to look at something and their hands start moving: a little sculling, a flick, a hand on a rock. It feels like control. It's really a sign that something else isn't working.

Your hands are compensating. Fix what they're compensating for, and they'll stop on their own.

## Why do hands creep in?

- **Feet that sink.** If your trim is feet-down, your hands paddle to keep you level.
- **Too much weight.** Extra lead means extra air in the wing, which makes you less stable. Hands steady the wobble.
- **Breathing that moves you.** If each breath lifts and drops you, hands chase the movement.
- **No other way to move.** If you can't back up or turn with your fins, your hands become your steering.
- **Habit.** Many divers were never told to keep their hands still. It's easy to fix once you notice.

## How does UTD score buoyancy?

UTD's class evaluation parameters give a clear picture of what good looks like:

| Score | Buoyancy |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Gross buoyancy failure |
| 2 | More than about 1 meter (3 feet) of variation, or using hands for buoyancy |
| 3 | Less than about 1 meter of variation |
| 4 | Less than about 30 centimeters (1 foot) of variation |
| 5 | Less than about 10 centimeters (4 inches) of variation |

A UTD certification requires at least a 3 in every evaluated section. Notice where hands sit: using them for buoyancy is a 2, however close you stay to your depth. Your instructor scores buoyancy in the context of the task, the course and the team, not as a single number in isolation.

## Fix the causes first

1. **Weight.** Do an end-of-dive weight check and drop any extra lead. See [How Much Weight Do I Need?](/learn/how-much-weight-do-i-need-scuba)
2. **Trim.** Knees bent, fins up, head forward. If your feet sink when you stop, see [Horizontal Trim](/learn/horizontal-trim-feet-sinking).
3. **Breathing.** Hold your depth by adjusting your average lung volume, not by gulping or pausing. See [Breathing for Buoyancy](/learn/breathing-for-buoyancy).
4. **Fin control.** Learn the back kick and helicopter turn, so your fins handle small position changes. See [Scuba Finning Techniques](/learn/scuba-finning-techniques).

## Drills to keep your hands still

**Give your hands a job.** Clasp them in front of you, or hold your primary light. Holding something removes the temptation.

**Hover on a line.** Stop beside a fixed line or over sand at a set depth. Hold it for 30 seconds, hands clasped. Build to two minutes.

**Add a task.** Once you can hover, read your gauge, check your computer or signal a teammate without moving your hands or changing depth. Real dives ask for control while you're doing something else.

**Turn and back up.** From the hover, turn 180 degrees with a helicopter turn and back up a few feet with a back kick, hands still the whole time.

**Film it.** Hands move without you noticing. Ten seconds of video from the side will show you every flick.

## What if you start to drift?

Stay calm and correct with the right tool. Small depth changes: breathing. Bigger depth changes: a small adjustment to the wing, then back to breathing. Position or angle: your fins. Hands stay where they are.

If you keep reaching for the bottom or a rock, move higher in the water. Practice where there's nothing to grab.
