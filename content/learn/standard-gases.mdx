Ask a DIR diver what's in their cylinder and the answer is usually one of a handful of mixes. That's not a lack of imagination. It's one of UTD's ten covenants, and it does a lot of quiet work for the team.

## What is the standard gas rule?

UTD's fourth covenant: dive the desired oxygen partial pressure at the target average depth, and keep the equivalent narcotic depth to 100 feet or less. No deep air.

Two limits sit behind it:

- **Oxygen.** UTD's standard bottom mixes are chosen for an average working oxygen partial pressure of 1.2 and a maximum of 1.4. Decompression mixes are used up to 1.6.
- **Narcosis.** The equivalent narcotic depth stays at 100 feet or less. UTD's calculation treats oxygen as narcotic, so only helium reduces it.

UTD also caps gas density for training dives at 5.7 grams per liter, because dense gas is harder to breathe at depth.

## What are UTD's standard gases?

**Bottom mixes**

| Mix | Working depth | Maximum operating depth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nitrox 32 | 0 to 100 ft | 111 ft |
| Trimix 25/25 | 100 to 130 ft | 151 ft |
| Trimix 21/35 | 130 to 160 ft | 190 ft |
| Trimix 18/45 | 170 to 200 ft | 220 ft |
| Trimix 15/55 | 210 to 240 ft | 275 ft |
| Trimix 12/60 | 250 to 300 ft | 352 ft |
| Trimix 10/70 | 310 to 360 ft | 429 ft |

The working-depth column reflects the [current UTD standard-gas chart](https://utdscubadiving.com/courses/utd-standards-and-procedures/lessons/standards-and-procedures-section-1-0-overview/topic/1-4-2-equivalent-narcotic-and-gas-density-limits/), not an operational or certification depth limit. The MOD column is an oxygen-pressure limit, not permission to exceed the working range, narcotic-depth limit, gas-density limit or course qualification. For example, nitrox 32's listed MOD does not authorize a UTD air/nitrox dive beyond 100 feet. Hypoxic mixes are not suitable for breathing at the surface; their use requires the appropriate training and travel-gas plan.

**Decompression mixes**

| Mix | Maximum depth |
| --- | --- |
| Oxygen (100%) | 20 ft |
| Nitrox 50 | 70 ft |
| Trimix 35/25 | 120 ft |
| Trimix 21/35 | 190 ft |

Trimix numbers give oxygen first, then helium: 21/35 is 21 percent oxygen, 35 percent helium and the rest nitrogen.

## Why does the team use the same gas?

- **Gas sharing is predictable.** If a teammate donates, you're breathing the same mix you planned for, at the depth you planned for it.
- **Decompression plans line up.** Standard gases simplify a common ascent, but each diver's actual exposure and obligations still matter. Agree a plan that accommodates the whole team; matching mixes alone does not make decompression identical.
- **Planning is simpler.** With a short list of mixes, maximum depths and switch depths become familiar. Fewer numbers means fewer mistakes.
- **Logistics are simpler.** A short list of mixes helps teams arrange fills and analyze cylinders consistently. Blending is a separate trained task; every diver verifies the actual analyzed mix before use.
- **No deep air.** Keeping narcosis in check means the team can think clearly when something goes wrong, which is the whole point of a thinking team.

## Which gases does each course use?

| Course | Gases |
| --- | --- |
| Recreational 2 / Advanced | Nitrox 32 to 100 ft |
| Recreational 3 | Trimix 25/25 to 130 ft, within no-decompression limits |
| Essentials courses | Air or nitrox 32 to 60 ft (100 ft for nitrox-certified divers in Essentials of Rec) |
| Techreational | Trimix 25/25 to 130 ft, one oxygen deco bottle, limited to one oxygen cycle |
| Tech 1 | Nitrox 32 or appropriate trimix 25/25, 21/35 or 18/45; max 160 ft; one Nitrox 50 decompression cylinder; max 30 min total decompression |

In UTD's recreational Min Deco table, the entries between 110 and 130 feet apply to trimix 25/25 only. They don't authorize air or nitrox 32 at those depths.

## Does helium make a dive technical?

No. UTD's recreational training goes to 130 feet on 25/25 within no-decompression limits. What moves a dive into technical training is planned decompression, and overhead environments are their own category. The gas follows the depth; it doesn't define the dive.
