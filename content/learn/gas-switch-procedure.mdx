There's a moment at the end of a technical dive when everything you've done right can be undone in one breath.

You're at the switch depth. A regulator is in your hand. It looks right. It's the color you expected. You're tired, you're cold, and the stop clock is running.

If that regulator belongs to the wrong bottle, the next breath could be your last. A high-oxygen deco gas breathed too deep can trigger an oxygen toxicity seizure, with no warning, underwater.

So the gas switch isn't a step. It's a protocol. And the protocol is built so that "it looks right" never gets to decide.

## What does a gas switch have to confirm?

Four things. Every time.

- **Depth.** You're at the planned switch depth, within the gas's maximum operating depth.
- **The gas.** The bottle holds what you think it holds. The analysis label and the MOD marking say so, not your memory. See [Deco Bottle Marking](/learn/deco-bottle-marking).
- **The regulator.** The second stage in your hand is connected to that bottle. Not the one next to it.
- **The team.** Your teammates confirm the bottle and watch the switch.

If the team can't confirm any one of those, the switch doesn't happen.

The sequence that confirms them, in what order, with which hand, is what we drill in training. It's short. It's also the kind of thing that has to be learned in the water, with an instructor watching, until you can't do it any other way.

## Why don't DIR divers color-code regulators?

Walk around a technical dive boat and you'll see green hoses on oxygen regulators, colored tape and colored mouthpieces. It looks organized. It feels safe.

Here's the problem. Color doesn't confirm anything the switch needs to confirm.

- **Color doesn't tell you what's in the bottle.** The analysis label and the MOD marking do.
- **Color doesn't know your depth.** Your depth gauge does.
- **Regulators move.** If your deco and stage regulators are all set up for oxygen service, any of them can go on any bottle. A color tied to one gas stops meaning anything the first time you swap.
- **Color gives false confidence.** "It's the green one" is exactly the shortcut a tired diver takes, and exactly the one that kills when a hose or a bottle got swapped on the boat.

Let's be honest. A colored hose won't hurt you on its own. Trusting it will.

## Why one diver at a time?

Because the team is the check. If everyone switches at once, nobody is watching. One diver switches while the others confirm the bottle and watch. Then the next.

It takes a little longer. That's the point.

## How do you get good at gas switches?

You practice them until the sequence is boring. On every training dive. With a teammate watching. With a bottle that's actually been analyzed and marked.

Then you practice them tired, cold and task-loaded, because that's when you'll do them for real.

Gas switching, bottle handling and passing bottles between divers are the core of the [Stage and/or Deco Bottle Extension](/courses/stage-and-or-deco-bottle-extension). To check a mix's MOD before the dive, use the [nitrox and trimix calculator](/tools/nitrox-trimix-calculator).

So, next switch, what will you check first? The color? Or the bottle?
