In the DIR configuration, you don't have an "octopus" somewhere on your chest. You have a backup regulator on a necklace, right under your chin, always in the same place. It's the regulator you'll breathe from the moment you donate your primary.

## What is a bungeed backup?

Your backup second stage sits on a short hose, held by a loop of bungee cord or surgical tubing that goes around your neck. The loop keeps the regulator just under your chin, so you can take it the instant you need it.

It works together with the long hose. You breathe from the primary on a long hose and donate that one to a teammate who's out of gas. Then you take the backup with your left hand and breathe. See [Long Hose Configuration](/learn/long-hose-configuration).

## Why wear it on a necklace?

- **It's always in the same place.** You never search for it, even in the dark or without a mask.
- **It's ready immediately.** The moment your primary leaves your mouth, your backup is right there.
- **It stays clean.** It isn't dragging through sand or banging on the bottom.
- **It's out of the way.** Nothing dangles, nothing snags.

## How do you set it up?

**The hose.** Gardner Underwater's equipment list specifies a 22- to 24-inch hose for the backup. On a single tank it comes from the left side of the first stage; on backmount doubles it comes from the left post. It should reach your mouth with your head turned, without loops hanging below your chin.

**The necklace.** A loop of bungee cord or surgical tubing, secured to the regulator just behind the mouthpiece. Many divers use a small cable tie or a tight wrap to hold it in place.

**The size.** The loop should be big enough to go over your head and let the regulator come to your mouth easily, but small enough that the regulator sits right under your chin rather than hanging at your chest. Try it on with your exposure suit and hood.

**The regulator's position.** With the necklace on, the regulator should sit the right way up, ready to go straight into your mouth. Ask your regulator technician or instructor to set up the second stage and hose for the left-side routing.

## How do you check it before the dive?

- **Breathe it** on land and in the water, with your eyes on the pressure gauge.
- **Check the bungee** for cracks, stretching or a loose attachment.
- **Check the mouthpiece** for tears or bite-through.
- **Look for bubbles** during the bubble check at the start of the descent.

## What can go wrong?

| Problem | What happens | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Necklace too loose | Regulator hangs at your chest and you have to search for it | Shorten the loop |
| Necklace too tight | Uncomfortable, and the regulator pulls your head | Lengthen the loop slightly |
| Perished bungee | The loop snaps and the regulator drops away | Replace bungee regularly |
| Hose too long | A loop hangs below your chin and snags | Use the specified hose length |
| Torn mouthpiece | Hard to breathe from cleanly | Replace it |
| Free-flow | Loud loss of gas from the backup | Practiced response, taught in training |
