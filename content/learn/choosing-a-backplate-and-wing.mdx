A backplate and wing has three parts: the plate, the wing and the harness. Each is a separate decision, which is the point: you can change one without replacing the others. It also means there are a few choices to get right.

## Quick decision table

| Your diving | Plate | Wing | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Single aluminum cylinder, warm water, thin wetsuit | Aluminum or light steel | Compact single-tank wing | Travel-friendly; you may need little or no extra lead |
| Single aluminum cylinder, cold water, 7 mm wetsuit | Stainless steel | Single-tank wing sized for the suit | Steel plate carries ballast you'd otherwise wear on a belt |
| Single cylinder in a drysuit | Usually stainless steel | Single-tank wing | Undergarments add lift, so redo your weight check |
| Backmount doubles | Depends on cylinders and suit | Doubles wing | Steel doubles go with a drysuit |

In my classes, steel cylinders never go with a wetsuit, whatever the plate.

## Steel or aluminum plate?

**Stainless steel** plates are heavier, typically around 5 to 6 pounds. In cold water, with a thick wetsuit or a drysuit, that weight replaces lead you'd otherwise carry on a belt, and it sits high and centered on your back. It moves weight rather than reducing the total, so you still keep some weight you can ditch. See [What Is a Balanced Rig](/learn/balanced-rig).

**Aluminum** plates are light. They suit warm water, thin suits and travel, where you don't need the extra weight and don't want to carry it in your luggage.

If you dive both in cold water at home and warm water on trips, a steel plate at home and an aluminum plate for travel is a common combination. The harness moves between them.

## How much wing lift do you need?

Enough to do two jobs, with a margin:

1. **Float your full rig at the surface**, with full cylinders.
2. **Hold the full rig at depth.** Full cylinders are heavy, and a wetsuit loses lift as it compresses. The wing has to make up the difference.

Then stop. The temptation is to buy a big wing "just in case," but an oversized wing traps air in its corners, is harder to vent and adds drag. Size it to the rig you actually dive, not the biggest dive you can imagine.

Single-tank and doubles wings are different products. One wing rarely does both well.

## What harness?

A DIR harness is simple on purpose:

- **One continuous piece of 2-inch webbing**, threaded through the plate.
- **A single buckle** at the waist.
- **A crotch strap**, so the plate can't ride up.
- **D-rings** at the chest and on the left side of the waist belt. We use adjustable chest D-rings and two adjustable D-rings on the left hip.
- **No quick releases and no padding.**

It's adjusted once to your body and your suit, and checked again whenever your suit changes. Snug enough to hold the plate in place, loose enough to reach your valves and breathe deeply.

## Other details worth getting right

- **Inflator on the left.** The wing's inflator hose comes over your left shoulder, matching the standard configuration.
- **A single-tank adapter** if you dive a single cylinder on a flat plate, unless your wing is designed to hold one directly.
- **Cam bands** that hold the cylinder high enough to reach the valve, without it hitting your head.
- **Nothing extra.** No pockets on the wing, no D-rings you won't use.
