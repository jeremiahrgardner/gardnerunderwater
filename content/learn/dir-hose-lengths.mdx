A hose that's two inches too long doesn't look like a problem on the bench.

In the water, it's a loop. A loop is a snag. And a snag at the wrong moment is a problem you didn't plan for.

Too short is just as bad. It pulls your head when you turn. It fights you when you reach for a valve. It turns a simple move into a wrestling match.

## What length does UTD set?

One that matters more than all the others: the **long-hose primary is 7 feet**. UTD's single-tank standard specifies it, and we use the same length on doubles. It's the regulator you donate, and it has to let two divers swim, ascend and get out together. The full argument is in [5 ft vs 7 ft Long Hose](/learn/5-ft-vs-7-ft-long-hose).

## What about every other hose?

They're fitted, not looked up.

The necklace backup, the pressure gauge, the wing inflator, the drysuit inflator: the right length for each depends on your body, your exposure suit and where the ports sit on your first stage. A hose that's perfect in a thin wetsuit can pull in a drysuit with a thick hood. A gauge hose that's right on one first stage can be wrong on another.

That's why we don't publish a chart of numbers and call it a standard. We fit the rig to the diver, in class, wearing the gear they'll dive in.

## What does a good fit look like?

The same rule for every hose: **the shortest length that works without pulling.**

- **The backup** reaches your mouth with your head turned, from right under your chin. No loop.
- **The gauge** reads easily at your left hip without unclipping, with nothing dangling.
- **The inflators** let you turn your head, reach your valves and work the inflator with no tension.
- **Nothing stands out** from your outline, from the side or from behind.

## Isn't there a chart for this?

Here's the reality check.

**The comfortable belief:** there's a chart. Match the chart and you're done.

**The truth:** a chart gets you close. Your body, your suit and your first stage do the rest. And a diver who's fighting a hose isn't thinking about their team.

## Which hose is the odd one out?

Look at your rig in the mirror, in full kit. Which hose loops? Which one pulls? Which one have you been tucking, twisting or putting up with for years?
