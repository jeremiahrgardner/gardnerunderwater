Look at a typical rental regulator. A yellow octopus on a long loop. A console swinging off the side. Hoses pointing every direction.

It works. Until you need it to work fast.

The problem isn't the gear. It's that nothing lives in a fixed place. And a regulator you have to look for is a regulator you can't count on.

## What does the single tank layout look like?

One first stage. Five hoses. Every one of them has a job and a place.

| Hose | Side | Where it goes | Typical length |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Long-hose primary | Right | Down your right side, retained at the hip, around your neck | 7 ft (UTD allows 5 or 7) |
| Necklace backup | Left | Over your left shoulder to the necklace under your chin | About 22 in |
| Wing inflator | Left | Up to the inflator on your left shoulder | Supplied with the wing |
| Pressure gauge | Left | Down your left side to the left hip D-ring | About 26 in |
| Drysuit inflator | Either | Under your arm to the suit valve | Short, fitted to the rig |

Off-the-shelf single tank hose kits look exactly like that: a 7-foot primary, a 22-inch backup and a 26-inch gauge hose ([example kit](https://extreme-exposure.com/single-tank-hose-kit)). Gardner Underwater's equipment list specifies the 7-foot primary.

## How should each hose run?

**Set the first stage first.** Mount it so the long hose port faces your right side and every hose can leave without a sharp bend. Many first stages have a swivel turret that makes this easy. On fixed-port first stages, pick ports by where each hose needs to go, not by which port is closest.

**The long hose.** Right-side port. It drops straight down your right side, gets retained under your light canister or tucked in your waist belt at the hip, crosses your chest and wraps around your neck into your mouth from the right. That's the regulator you donate. [Long Hose Configuration](/learn/long-hose-configuration) walks through the donation.

**The backup.** Left-side port, over your left shoulder, held under your chin by a bungee necklace. Because its hose arrives from the left, the second stage may need to be set up for left-side routing. Ask your regulator technician. See [Bungeed Backup Regulator](/learn/bungeed-backup-regulator).

**The wing inflator.** Left-side port, straight up to the inflator on your left shoulder. No loop over the top of the valve.

**The pressure gauge.** High-pressure port, down your left side, clipped to the left hip D-ring. You should read it without unclipping it.

**The drysuit hose.** A spare low-pressure port. Which side depends on your first stage. What matters is the result: a short run under your arm to the suit valve, close to your body, with nothing looping out.

## Why does the long hose go on the right?

Because of what happens next.

You take the regulator out of your mouth and hand it to a teammate who's out of gas. It comes in from the right, so it presents straight to them, right way up. Your left hand goes to your chin. Your backup is there. You breathe.

The whole layout is built around those few seconds. Right hand donates. Left hand finds the backup. Everything else stays out of the way.

## Is this overkill on a single tank?

Let's be honest. A lot of divers think DIR routing is for tech divers. Doubles. Caves. Not a Saturday at La Jolla Shores.

**The comfortable belief:** I'm a recreational diver, so a recreational setup is fine.

**The truth:** an out-of-gas emergency doesn't check your certification card first.

And here's the part people miss. The single tank is where you build the habit. The layout you learn on one first stage is the one you'll use on [doubles](/learn/doubles-hose-routing), with stages and on a scooter. Learn it once. Then just keep adding. [DIR for Recreational Divers](/learn/dir-for-recreational-divers) makes the case in full.

## What should you check before every dive?

- **Long hose on the right**, free to deploy in one movement.
- **Backup under your chin**, not hanging at your chest.
- **Gauge clipped** at the left hip, readable without unclipping.
- **Inflator connected** and working.
- **No loops** standing out from your outline, front or back.
- **Breathe both regulators** while you watch the gauge.

[Pre-Dive Checks](/learn/pre-dive-checks) covers the full routine.

## Can you set it up from memory?

Pull your regulator out of the bag tonight. Lay it on the floor. Where does each hose go? Why there? Which hand reaches for which regulator, and when?

If you can answer all of that without thinking, good. If you can't, start with [Essentials of Recreational Diving](/courses/essentials-of-recreational-diving). It's where the layout stops being a picture and becomes a habit.
