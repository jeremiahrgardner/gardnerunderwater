# Gardner Underwater — DIR Content & Keyword Plan

Oct 14, 2026 · @Jeremiah

## Summary

Publish 48 articles across eight topic clusters in three waves. Start with 14: seven pillar guides and seven DIR questions where today's top results are mostly old forum threads.

- **The opening.** For almost every DIR-specific query checked (long hose, Hogarthian rig, bungeed backup, back kick, rock bottom gas, UTD vs GUE, "is DIR elitist"), page one is dominated by years-old ScubaBoard threads, Wikipedia, gear retailers and agency course pages. Current, instructor-written explainers are rare.
- **Why it matters twice.** AI answer engines draw on the same pool. A clear, dated, instructor-authored page is well placed to become the source they cite for "what is a long hose" or "how do I back kick."
- **The harder ground.** Broad skill topics (buoyancy, weighting, air consumption) have far more searchers but face scubadiving.com, scuba.com, SDI/TDI, DAN and Dive Magazine. Win those with the DIR system angle and your own video, not by out-publishing them.
- **Local gap.** Nobody owns "DIR" or "technical diving" content for San Diego; results are a shop aggregator and forum threads.
- **Every article sells something you already teach.** Each one links to a course on /courses: ESM, Essentials of Rec, Essentials of Tec, the Back Kick and Propulsion MINIs, Doubles, Drysuit, SMB, or coaching.
- **Fix the site first.** Every page shares the same title and meta description, there is no blog section, and courses have no individual pages to link to. See Site fixes.

Demand ratings are estimates from topic breadth and what ranks today. Semrush is connected, but the account had no API units left to pull volumes; once it does, every row gets real monthly numbers.

## Strategy

Lead with the problems certified divers already search for, then show how the DIR system solves them. Ideology second, fixes first.

| Audience | What they search | What they need | Where they convert |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Certified rec divers who feel stuck (largest pool) | "feet sink scuba," "how much weight do I need," "improve air consumption" | A fix that works on the next dive | ESM, Essentials of Rec, coaching |
| DIR-curious divers | "long hose," "backplate vs jacket BCD," "what is DIR diving" | A fair, plain explanation and a gear path | Essentials of Rec, Shop |
| Aspiring tech divers | "how to become a technical diver," "UTD vs GUE," "gradient factors" | An honest path with readiness markers | Essentials of Tec, Doubles |
| San Diego divers | "technical diving San Diego," "La Jolla Shores diving" | Where to train, when to dive dry | Any course, booked locally |

**Why DIR topics are winnable.** The ranking pages are mostly forum debates: long, contradictory and undated. A searcher has to read 40 replies to get an answer. An article that answers in the first 50 words, then explains why, beats that for the reader, for Google and for AI engines looking for a quotable definition.

**Tone is a competitive edge.** The most common objection across the forums is that DIR divers are rigid or arrogant. Your existing language ("Thinking Diver," "Eyes Open. Brain On. Unified Team.") already answers that. Every article should read as an invitation, never a correction.

**How the clusters connect.** Each cluster has one pillar guide and several focused articles. Links run one way toward a booking:

1. A focused article answers one question ("how do I back kick?").
2. It links up to its pillar ("Scuba Finning Techniques") and across to two siblings.
3. The pillar and the article both end with one course link (Back Kick MINI).
4. The course page ends with Book Your Course.

## AI-friendly article template

Write every article so its first 50 words fully answer the question. Google's May 2026 guidance says AI Overviews and AI Mode run on its core ranking systems, so this is good SEO first, not a separate trick ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features), via [Pepper](https://www.pepper.inc/blog/generative-engine-optimization/)).

**On the page, in this order**

1. **Title** phrased the way divers ask it: "How to Back Kick: Step by Step."
2. **Answer block**: 40–60 words right under the title that answer the question on their own. This is the passage engines lift.
3. **Why it matters**: two or three sentences tied to safety, comfort or gas.
4. **Question-shaped H2s**, each opening with a one-sentence answer to itself. Engines extract from the top of a section, not the middle.
5. **A table** wherever readers compare options (backplate vs jacket, GUE vs UTD, 5-ft vs 7-ft hose).
6. **Your original material**: a Dive Table clip, a photo of your rig, a worked gas example with real numbers, a drill you use with students. Unique evidence is what makes you the cited source rather than one of many.
7. **Common mistakes and fixes.**
8. **Short FAQ**: three to five questions lifted from real forum threads.
9. **One course link** matched to the article.
10. **Author box and date**: Jeremiah Gardner, credentials, San Diego, links to About and jeremiahgardner.com, plus a visible "Last reviewed" date.

**Behind the page**

- Article and Person structured data on every post; VideoObject where a clip is embedded. Keep it basic: Google's guidance warns against structured-data over-optimization and dismisses llms.txt and AI-specific rewrites.
- Check robots.txt doesn't block AI crawlers (OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, Googlebot). Content already renders as plain HTML, which is what crawlers need.
- Describe the business the same way everywhere (site, Google Business Profile, YouTube, Instagram): "DIR-based scuba training and coaching in San Diego, led by Jeremiah Gardner."

**Off the page**

Muck Rack's May 2026 analysis of 25 million AI-cited links found 84% came from earned media, not brand sites ([Muck Rack](https://muckrack.com/blog/what-is-ai-reading-may-2026), via [Pepper](https://www.pepper.inc/blog/generative-engine-optimization/)). So pair publishing with mentions: answer ScubaBoard and r/scuba questions with the relevant article where it genuinely helps, guest on other dive podcasts, and pitch pieces to X-Ray Mag, DAN's Alert Diver and California Diver.

## Site fixes before publishing

Six fixes give the articles somewhere to send readers and stop the site's own pages competing with each other.

- [ ] **Unique titles and meta descriptions.** The home, Courses and Training pages all carry the title "Gardner Underwater" and the same description. Give each page its own, e.g. "DIR Scuba Courses in San Diego | Gardner Underwater."
- [ ] **One page per course.** Courses currently share one page and every Book Now goes to Contact. Build individual pages for at least ESM, Essentials of Rec, Essentials of Tec, Doubles, Back Kick MINI, Propulsion MINI, Drysuit, SMB MINI and Coaching. Each becomes a link target and can rank for "\[course\] San Diego."

- [ ] **Fix visible typos.** "dissapointed," "hightest" (home), "Tranistion" (Courses), "netural" (Training). Small, but trust signals count for instructional content.
- [ ] **Make About the author page.** Credentials, agencies, years teaching, photo, links to jeremiahgardner.com, YouTube and X. Every article's author box points here.
- [ ] **Google Business Profile.** If it isn't set up and verified, do that before the San Diego articles go live; local results lean on it.

## Measurement

Validate demand before Wave 2, then judge the program on impressions, AI citations and course inquiries rather than raw traffic.

- **Validate volumes.** Semrush is connected; once the account has API units, I can pull monthly volume and difficulty for every row and rewrite the Demand column with real numbers. Free fallback: Google Keyword Planner ranges.
- **Search Console.** Track impressions and average position per article. Impressions move before clicks; a page stuck on page two after a few months needs a rewrite or better internal links.
- **AI citations.** Once a month, ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google AI Mode the same 20 core questions ("what is a long hose configuration," "how do I back kick") and log whether gardnerunderwater.com is named or cited. Bing Webmaster Tools' free AI Performance report shows Copilot citations ([Bing](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/February-2026/Introducing-AI-Performance-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Public-Preview)).
- **Bookings.** Track Book Now and Contact clicks per article, and add "How did you find us?" to the contact form.
- **Refresh.** Re-review each pillar every six months and update its "Last reviewed" date.
